I personally do not think we are completely defined by our genome. Our genes play a big role in how our bodies develop and can affect our chances of developing certain diseases. However, I think our environment and the experiences we go through also play a big role in who we become. Our DNA can tell us a lot about our biology, but it cannot tell the whole story about us.

One example is how having a genetic risk for a disease does not always mean you will develop it. MedlinePlus explains that some people have a genetic variant associated with a condition but never show signs of it. This is called reduced penetrance. For example, harmful variants in the BRCA1 and BRCA2 genes increase the risk of certain cancers, but not everyone with these variants develops cancer [1]. To me, this shows that genes can affect what might happen without always determining what will happen.

Another thing to consider is how our environment affects the way our genes work. According to the CDC, our behaviors and environment can cause changes in gene activity without changing the DNA sequence itself. This is called epigenetics [2]. So, even though we inherit our DNA, other factors can still influence how those genetic instructions are used in our bodies.

I also think there is more to a person than their physical traits or their risk of disease. The way we grow up, the people around us, and the challenges we go through all help shape our perspective. I would not describe myself only through my genetics because that would leave out my values, goals, and experiences.

Overall, I think our genome is part of what makes us who we are, but it does not completely define us. It helps explain our biology, while our environment and experiences also help shape the people we become.
