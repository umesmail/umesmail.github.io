# 23andMe

23andMe was founded in 2006 by Anne Wojcicki, Linda Avey, and Paul Cusenza with the goal of making genetic information more accessible to everyday people. The company received about $9 million in its first major Series A investment round in 2007, with investors including Google, Genentech, New Enterprise Associates, and Mohr Davidow Ventures.

23andMe became best known for its direct-to-consumer DNA testing kits. Customers provide a saliva sample, which is analyzed to give them information about their ancestry, genetic traits, and certain health-related genetic risks. The company also built a large genetic research database using information from customers who consented to participate in research, allowing genetic data to contribute to studies and drug development.

Despite becoming one of the most recognizable companies in consumer genetics, 23andMe experienced major financial difficulties. For the fiscal year ending March 31, 2025, the company reported approximately $189.9 million in revenue, down from about $219.6 million the previous year. In March 2025, 23andMe filed for bankruptcy after struggling financially. Later in 2025, TTAM Research Institute, led by co-founder Anne Wojcicki, acquired substantially all of 23andMe's assets for $305 million.

Going forward, 23andMe's future will depend on whether its genetic database and testing services can continue to contribute to health research while maintaining customers' trust and protecting their genetic information.
